LEGAL SUPPORT · AFRICA

Africa

Organizations and networks that may help families affected by childhood cancer understand patient rights, access healthcare, find family support, connect with advocacy organizations, and navigate healthcare and social systems across Africa.

LEGAL RESOURCES

Support for families.

The organizations below work on childhood cancer care, family support, healthcare access, advocacy, policy, and rights-related issues across different parts of Africa. Some provide direct services to families, while others focus on healthcare systems, advocacy, information, referrals, or professional networks. Always check the organization's official website for current services and eligibility.

01

GFAOP →

The Franco-African Group of Paediatric Oncology is a network of childhood cancer specialists working across 18 countries in North Africa and sub-Saharan Africa. Its activities include supporting pediatric oncology units, helping families, and advocacy for better access to childhood cancer care.

Africa
18 countries
02

Childhood Cancer International · Africa Network →

Childhood Cancer International connects parent and childhood cancer organizations around the world. Its African member network includes organizations supporting families in countries such as Ghana, Ethiopia, Kenya, Morocco, Niger, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and others.

Africa
Regional network
03

CHOC Childhood Cancer Foundation SA →

A South African nonprofit advocating for children and teenagers with cancer or life-threatening blood disorders and their families. CHOC provides psychosocial, emotional, practical, accommodation, transport, and family support, while also advocating for the rights and well-being of childhood cancer patients.

South Africa
04

CANSA · The Cancer Association of South Africa →

A South African cancer organization providing cancer information, patient support, advocacy, and practical resources. CANSA also provides information and support relating to childhood cancer and can help families find cancer-related services in South Africa.

South Africa
05

Hope for Cancer Kids →

A Kenyan nonprofit supporting children with cancer and their caregivers. Its programs include medical access, health insurance support, transport, accommodation, nutrition, psychosocial care, and advocacy aimed at reducing barriers to completing treatment.

Kenya
06

Mathiwos Wondu Foundation →

An Ethiopian cancer organization founded by the family of a child who died from leukemia. The foundation began with a focus on pediatric cancer and continues to support cancer patients and families while working to strengthen cancer care and public health systems in Ethiopia.

Ethiopia
07

WHO Global Initiative for Childhood Cancer →

The World Health Organization's global initiative works with countries and partners to improve childhood cancer care, strengthen health systems, increase access to essential medicines, and reduce inequalities. It also supports governments in developing stronger national childhood cancer programs.

Global
WHO resource
08

WHO Regional Office for Africa →

The WHO Regional Office for Africa provides health information, regional policy guidance, and resources for African countries. Its work includes childhood cancer initiatives, strengthening health systems, improving access to treatment, and supporting national cancer programs.

Africa
WHO region
09

WHO Africa · Cancer Resources →

Regional WHO resources on cancer prevention, treatment, health-system strengthening, and access to care in African countries. Families can use these resources to understand broader healthcare initiatives and locate information relevant to national cancer services.

Africa
WHO resource

Childhood Cancer International →

A global network of childhood cancer parent and patient organizations. Its African member organizations provide a useful starting point for families looking for local support groups, advocacy organizations, and family assistance in different African countries.

Global
Africa network

IARC · Childhood Cancer Resources →

The International Agency for Research on Cancer provides international information and research on childhood cancer, including data on cancer burden and inequalities in diagnosis and treatment. These resources can help families and advocates understand the wider context of childhood cancer in Africa.

Global
WHO / IARC

South African Children's Cancer Study Group →

A South African pediatric oncology network providing information and connections for patients, parents, healthcare professionals, and childhood cancer support organizations. Its patient and parent resources can help families identify appropriate support during treatment.

South Africa
BEFORE REACHING OUT

A note about legal support

Legal rights, public healthcare systems, insurance rules, education protections, social benefits, and available family services vary significantly between African countries. Some organizations listed here provide direct family assistance, while others focus on advocacy, healthcare access, information, policy, or referrals. Before contacting an organization, visit its official website and check which services are currently available for your family and location.

AFRICA

You don't have to navigate this alone.

Your child's healthcare team, hospital social worker, patient advocate, or local childhood cancer organization may also be able to help your family understand available rights, benefits, and local support resources.
