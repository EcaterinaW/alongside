LEGAL SUPPORT · AFRICA

Africa

Legal aid services, children's rights organizations, and advocacy resources that may help families affected by childhood cancer understand their rights, access legal assistance, and navigate healthcare, education, family, and social-support systems across Africa.

LEGAL RESOURCES

Support for families.

The organizations below include government legal-aid services, legal clinics, children's rights organizations, and advocacy resources. Some can provide legal advice, mediation, or representation, while others help families understand rights and connect with appropriate services. Always check eligibility and current services on the official website.

01

National Legal Aid Service (NLAS) →

Kenya's national legal-aid service provides assistance in civil, criminal, children's, constitutional, and public-interest matters. It also promotes legal awareness and advocacy for vulnerable and marginalized people. Families dealing with disputes involving children, healthcare, benefits, or other legal matters can use the service to understand available options.

Kenya
02

Legal Aid Commission Ghana →

Ghana's government Legal Aid Commission provides free legal consultation and advice for eligible people, together with legal representation, mediation, and paralegal services. It is intended to improve access to justice for poor and vulnerable people.

Ghana
03

Legal Aid South Africa →

A state-funded legal-aid organization providing legal advice and representation to eligible people who cannot afford private legal services. South Africa's government specifically identifies children among the groups that Legal Aid South Africa assists.

South Africa
04

Justice Centres Uganda →

A government legal-aid project providing free legal services to poor, vulnerable, and marginalized people. Services include legal advice, mediation, court representation, legal awareness, and support with self-representation.

Uganda
05

Access to Justice Bureaus (MAJ) →

Rwanda's Ministry of Justice operates Access to Justice Bureaus at district level. They provide free legal advice, legal assistance, and court representation for poor and vulnerable people, as well as mediation and other access-to-justice services.

Rwanda
06

Legal Aid Board of Zambia →

Zambia's Legal Aid Board provides legal advice and assistance to vulnerable and low-income people. Its services include civil legal aid and family matters such as child custody and child maintenance, as well as other civil and constitutional matters.

Zambia
07

Legal Aid Directorate · Namibia →

The Namibian Ministry of Justice provides legal aid to people who cannot afford private legal representation. Services include legal advice and representation in criminal, civil, and family matters, with children and other vulnerable groups among those who may benefit.

Namibia
08

Legal Aid Forum Rwanda →

A Rwandan legal-aid organization working to improve access to justice through legal assistance, legal information, community-based paralegals, and advocacy. Its services can help vulnerable people understand legal rights and available remedies.

Rwanda
09

Legal and Human Rights Centre · Legal Aid →

Tanzania's Legal and Human Rights Centre provides legal information and operates legal-aid clinics. Its online self-help resources include materials on children's law, child maintenance, family matters, and other legal issues that families may need to understand.

Tanzania
10

Childhood Cancer International →

A global network of childhood cancer parent and patient organizations. Its African member organizations can help families find local advocacy groups and support organizations that may provide guidance on healthcare access, patient rights, and navigating local systems.

Global
Africa network
11

CHOC Childhood Cancer Foundation SA →

A South African childhood cancer organization providing practical and psychosocial support to children and families. CHOC also advocates for the rights and well-being of children with cancer and can be a useful starting point for families seeking cancer-specific support and referrals.

South Africa
12

WHO Global Initiative for Childhood Cancer →

The World Health Organization's global childhood cancer initiative works with countries and partners to improve access to quality childhood cancer care, strengthen health systems, and reduce inequalities. It provides useful background for understanding national and regional childhood cancer policies and healthcare access.

Global
WHO resource
BEFORE REACHING OUT

A note about legal support

Legal rights, public healthcare systems, insurance rules, education protections, social benefits, and available family services vary significantly between African countries. Some organizations listed here provide direct legal advice, mediation, or representation, while others provide legal information, advocacy, referrals, or cancer-specific support. Eligibility requirements and available services can also vary. Before contacting an organization, visit its official website and check which services are currently available for your family and location.
