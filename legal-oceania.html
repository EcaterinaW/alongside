LEGAL SUPPORT · OCEANIA

Oceania

Organizations and services that may help families affected by childhood cancer understand patient rights, access legal and advocacy support, and navigate healthcare, financial, education, and family-related systems.

LEGAL RESOURCES

Support for families.

In Oceania, legal support connected to childhood cancer may include patient-rights information, legal referral services, advocacy, financial and workplace guidance, and organizations that help families navigate the wider impact of a child's diagnosis.

01

Cancer Council Australia · Pro Bono Program →

A national referral service connecting eligible people affected by cancer with volunteer lawyers, financial planners, and workplace professionals. Legal help may include employment, discrimination, insurance, debt, wills, and powers of attorney.

Australia
02

Community Legal Centres Australia →

A national network connecting people with local community legal centres. These centres provide free or low-cost legal and related services, including support with disadvantage, discrimination, money problems, and other legal issues.

Australia
03

Cancer Council Victoria · Legal Support →

Provides information about legal support services for people affected by cancer in Victoria. The service can help families find appropriate legal assistance for issues arising during or after cancer.

Victoria
Australia
04

Cancer Council NSW · Legal & Workplace Support →

Provides legal, financial, and workplace information for people affected by cancer. Resources include information about cancer care and rights, employment, insurance, planning ahead, and family-related issues.

New South Wales
Australia
05

Redkite →

A specialist childhood cancer support organization providing emotional, financial, and practical help to children and families. Redkite can also help families navigate other services and connect with appropriate support.

Australia
06

Camp Quality →

Supports Australian families affected by childhood cancer through family programs, practical support, counselling, education, and community connection. Its services are available to eligible families with children affected by cancer.

Australia
07

Cancer Council Australia · Childhood Cancer Support →

Provides information for families caring for a child with cancer, including support groups, practical services, financial assistance information, and connections to local Cancer Council services.

Australia
08

Child Cancer Foundation →

New Zealand's specialist childhood cancer family support organization. It provides personalised support, practical assistance, grants, information, and connections for children with cancer and their families across Aotearoa New Zealand.

New Zealand
09

Cancer Society NZ · Your Rights as a Patient →

Explains patient rights in New Zealand and points families toward the Health and Disability Commissioner when concerns arise about health or disability services. This can help families understand their rights within the healthcare system.

New Zealand
10

Cancer Society NZ · Advocacy →

The Cancer Society of New Zealand advocates for people affected by cancer and works to influence laws, policies, and healthcare systems. Its advocacy work can help families understand broader issues affecting access to cancer care.

New Zealand
11

Cancer Society NZ · Support & Navigation →

Provides information and support for people with cancer and their whānau, including access to Cancer Navigators in some regions. Families can use the service to identify appropriate local support and healthcare resources.

New Zealand
12

Children's Cancer Institute Australia →

An Australian childhood cancer research institute focused specifically on improving treatment and outcomes for children with cancer. Its work provides families with information about childhood cancer and the wider development of better care and treatment.

Australia
BEFORE REACHING OUT

A note about legal support

Legal and patient-rights support varies between countries and states across Oceania. In Australia, some cancer organizations can connect eligible families with lawyers or other professionals, while in New Zealand, families can find information about patient rights and healthcare advocacy through cancer organizations and official complaints processes. Always check the official website to confirm eligibility, location, and available services.

OCEANIA

You don't have to navigate this alone.

Your child's healthcare team, hospital social worker, patient advocate, childhood cancer organization, or cancer support service may also be able to help your family understand available rights, benefits, and local support resources.
