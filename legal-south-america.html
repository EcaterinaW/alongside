South America

Organizations and official resources that may help families affected by childhood cancer understand their rights, access benefits, navigate healthcare systems, and find legal or advocacy support across South America.

LEGAL RESOURCES

Support for families.

The resources below provide different forms of legal information, rights education, advocacy, benefits guidance, and family support. Some are government resources and others are organizations working directly with children with cancer and their families. Always check the official website for current requirements and available services.

01

Argentina — Childhood Cancer Rights →

Official government information about Law 27.674, which establishes a comprehensive protection system for children and adolescents with cancer, including rights related to healthcare, coverage, education, transport, housing, financial assistance, and caregiver leave.

Government
02

Instituto Nacional del Cáncer — Rights →

Official information from Argentina's National Cancer Institute explaining the rights provided by Law 27.674 and the National Program for Comprehensive Care of Children and Adolescents with Cancer.

Argentina
Government
03

Fundación Natalí Dafne Flexer →

A national nonprofit supporting children and young people with cancer and their families. The foundation helps families access treatment and support resources and works at local, national, and regional levels on issues affecting childhood cancer care.

Argentina
04

CONIACC →

Brazil's national confederation of childhood cancer support organizations. CONIACC represents affiliated institutions and explicitly works to defend the rights of children and adolescents with cancer and to guide member organizations on legal and rights-related matters.

Brazil
05

Brazil — Statute of the Rights of People with Cancer →

Federal legislation establishing rights for people with cancer, including access to social and legal assistance, priority, protection of social and economic wellbeing, and specific attention for children and adolescents with cancer.

Brazil
Government
06

Brazil — Ministry of Health →

Official guidance explaining access to cancer treatment through Brazil's public health system and the specific policy for children and adolescents with cancer, including specialized care and support for families.

Brazil
Government
07

Chile — Ley SANNA →

Official government information about the SANNA insurance system. Parents and eligible caregivers of children with cancer may receive protected leave from work and a subsidy while caring for their child during treatment.

Chile
Government
08

Corporación Oncomamás →

A Chilean organization created by parents of children with cancer. It provides family guidance and support and actively advocates for public policies, including the implementation and improvement of the SANNA law and access to childhood cancer treatment.

Chile
09

Fundación Nuestros Hijos →

Chilean childhood cancer organization providing integrated medical, rehabilitation, social, educational, and family programs while working to improve access and quality of care for children and young people with cancer.

Chile
10

Colombia — Ley Jacobo →

Official Colombian legislation protecting the health rights of children with cancer. The law establishes priority, comprehensive and continuous care and strengthens social support for minors with suspected or confirmed cancer.

Colombia
Government
11

Fundación Colombiana de Leucemia y Linfoma →

Provides free support to parents and caregivers of children with cancer in Colombia, including help addressing barriers to access the healthcare system, social support, education, and guidance throughout treatment.

Colombia
12

Colombia — Ministry of Health

Colombia — official government information about the rights of children with suspected or diagnosed cancer, including access to timely healthcare, diagnostic examinations, specialized oncology services, comprehensive treatment, and protection from copayments.

BEFORE REACHING OUT

A note about legal support

Legal rights, public benefits, caregiver leave, healthcare coverage, and available services vary between countries and may also depend on the family's circumstances. Some resources listed here provide direct family support or advocacy rather than legal representation. Before contacting an organization, visit its official website and check the current requirements, eligibility rules, and services available in your location.
